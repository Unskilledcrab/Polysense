# Nonpartisanship (draft)

Claiming to be unbiased isn't enough. We make fairness **structural, measurable and open to challenge.**

## Structural rules

1. **One template for everything.** Every workspace has the same layers, fields and order, whatever the
   topic or sponsor.
2. **Symmetry of structure, not of conclusions.**
   - Every option, including the status quo, gets the same fields: who benefits, who pays, what's
     uncertain.
   - Every option gets its strongest case for and against.
   - Evidence labels follow the evidence, so we never equalize ratings to look balanced.
3. **No endorsements, ratings, scorecards or "truth meters."**
4. **Neutral naming.** Use bill numbers and plain descriptions. Advocacy nicknames appear only in quotes.
   **Issues, not teams:** no party or left-right labels on people, arguments or organizations unless a
   fact can't be understood without one ([content-style-guide.md](../product/content-style-guide.md)).
5. **The loaded-word list** is enforced outside quotes ([content-style-guide.md](../product/content-style-guide.md)).
6. **Transparent topic selection.** A published rubric and a public coverage log, including what we
   *didn't* cover and the sponsor-party mix ([topic-selection.md](topic-selection.md)).

## Measuring it

- Track the **sponsor-party mix** of covered bills, published quarterly.
- **Bias panel.** Before a workspace is published, independent agents argue for each side's fair
  representation (a supporter's advocate and a critic's advocate). A standards editor runs a flip test.
  Each advocate must rate their side as fairly represented (at least 4/5). See the
  [verification protocol](../process/verification-protocol.md).
- **Real readers.** Readers' "Did this favor one side?" answers and public bias challenges are tracked
  by self-described leaning.
- Ask comprehension-round participants **"Did this favor one side?"** and log the answers by
  self-described leaning.
- Any bias challenge gets a public answer, even when the answer is "no change".

## IRS voter-education constraints

These apply once we are a 501(c)(3) or have a fiscal sponsor. Not legal advice; confirm with counsel.

- **No support for or opposition to any candidate,** in any form.
- **Candidate content**, if any, follows Rev. Rul. 78-248: identical questions to all candidates, broad
  issue coverage, neutral wording, and no editorial comment or ratings.
- **Voting records**, if any, follow Rev. Rul. 80-282: broad coverage, no editorial comment, no election
  targeting.
- **Timing.** Be extra careful with content that identifies candidates near elections (Rev. Rul.
  2007-41). Legislators are also future candidates.
- **No lobbying.** We explain how to participate. We never urge a position on a bill.
- **Election-season content** is reviewed against these rules before publication. The fiscal sponsor
  may require its own review.

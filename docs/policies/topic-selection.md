# Topic selection (draft)

We can't explain everything well, so we choose, and we show how we chose.

## Rubric (each criterion scored 0–3)

| Criterion | 0 | 3 |
|---|---|---|
| **Everyday impact** | Affects few people, or only technically | Directly affects many people's daily lives (costs, schools, housing, safety, health, utilities) |
| **Input still possible** | Already decided | A hearing, vote or signing decision is still ahead |
| **Evidence available** | Little to work with | Fiscal note, studies, other states' experience, official data |
| **Public confusion or attention** | Little interest or confusion | High attention, or widely misunderstood |
| **Likely to move** | Stalled | Has a hearing, passed a chamber, or has leadership support |

**Bonus +2:** proposed constitutional amendments, which become statewide ballot questions.

**Excluded:** local acts, honorary resolutions, and content centered on a candidate for office.

## Balance

Ties are broken to keep a **balanced portfolio across everyday-concern categories**:
- money and taxes;
- housing;
- schools and kids;
- health;
- jobs and business;
- safety and courts;
- roads and transit;
- energy and utilities;
- voting and government;
- rights.

**We never select or reject a bill because of its sponsor's party.**

## Coverage log (public)

Every candidate considered gets a row: bill, category, scores, decision (*covered*, *not now*,
*declined*), reason and date. The log includes the **sponsor-party mix** of covered bills. Anyone can
suggest a bill; suggestions are scored and logged too.

## Everything else

Bills without a deep workspace still get a **data-only card** (status, next step, links) labeled "not
yet explained", so nothing is hidden.

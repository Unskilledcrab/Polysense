# Naming

## Status

**"Civic Lens" can't be used as-is.** The code uses the codename **`civic`**. The public brand will be a
single setting.

What we found on 2026-11-08 (details in [research/landscape.md](../research/landscape.md#name-check)):

- **USPTO application "CIVICLENS"**, serial 99772668, Class 42, filed 2026-04-18, pending, by
  CivicLens LLC. Source: an aggregator site; **[unverified]** until checked in USPTO TSDR.
- **"CivicLens Legislation Tracker"** iOS app: a personalized bill feed with one-tap contact of
  representatives. This overlaps directly with our pilot.
- Other uses: an Esri partner called "CivicLens", a UChicago class project on federal comment periods,
  hackathon projects, and CIVICUS Lens.
- "Polysense", the old repository name, is used by an IoT sensor company and a food-tech AI startup.

## Criteria for the new name

1. Clear of conflicts in Class 42 (software/SaaS) and Class 41 (education/information), with a usable
   `.org` domain.
2. Plain and trustworthy rather than clever. Says "understand" or "participate", not "fight" or "win".
3. Politically neutral: no color, team or slogan associations.
4. Works for Georgia now and other states later. Avoid "Georgia" in the core name, but allow it in a
   tagline.
5. Easy to say, spell and search; short enough for a phone home screen.
6. Doesn't imply government affiliation (avoid ".gov"-like phrasing or "Official").

## Process

1. Brainstorm 20 names and score them against the criteria.
2. Run a **knockout search** on the top 5 in USPTO, app stores, GitHub, domain availability, and Georgia
   Secretary of State business filings.
3. Test the top 3 with comprehension-round participants: "What do you think this site does?"
4. Get **attorney clearance** for the winner before public beta.
5. Record the decision as an ADR.

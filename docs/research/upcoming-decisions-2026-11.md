## 3. PSC Docket 57171: how Georgia Power credits Real-Time Pricing revenue against fuel costs (kind 3)

**What is being decided:** how Georgia Power credits money from large customers on Real-Time Pricing (RTP) rates, including data centers, against the fuel costs that every customer pays. That decides how much of the fuel bill other customers carry.

**Who decides:** the five-member Georgia Public Service Commission, voting at its 2026-12-15 Administrative Session. The order reads: "A Final Decision on this proceeding will be made by the Commission on December 15, 2026 at the regularly scheduled Administrative Session." The case comes from a 2026-05-28 stipulation in the fuel case (Docket 56765), which said "This proceeding shall be concluded by December 31, 2026".

**Next public steps and how residents can take part:**
- **2026-11-12:** hearing on Georgia Power's rebuttal testimony, after the 9:30 a.m. committee meetings. The calendar lists 9:35 a.m.
- **2026-12-02, 4:00 p.m.:** briefs and proposed orders are due.
- **2026-12-10, 9:30 a.m.:** the Energy Committee considers the case.
- **2026-12-15:** final decision at the Administrative Session.
- **How to take part:**
  - Speak as a **public witness** at a hearing. The PSC page says to sign in about 30 minutes early: https://psc.ga.gov/about-the-psc/consumer-corner/consumer-advisories/public-witness/
  - File a **written public comment**: https://psc.ga.gov/public-comments/. The page says "Comments must be submitted 24 hours before a Commission vote." `{computed: a 9:30 a.m. vote on 2026-12-15, minus 24 hours = 9:30 a.m. on 2026-12-14}`
  - The project's research notes say public comment is allowed in the last 15 minutes of committee meetings. I could not confirm that on a PSC page **[unverified]**.
- The legal notice puts the hearings in the Commission offices at 244 Washington Street SW, Atlanta.

**Primary sources:**
- Docket page (opened 06/02/2026, status Open): https://psc.ga.gov/search/facts-docket/?docketId=57171
- Procedural and Scheduling Order, adopted 2026-07-07: https://psc.ga.gov/search/facts-document/?documentId=227135. Readable copy (.docx): https://services.psc.ga.gov/api/v1/External/Public/Get/Document/DownloadFile/227135/108426. The PDF copy is scanned, with no text layer.
- Hearing listing for 2026-11-12: https://psc.ga.gov/agendas-calendars/commission-calendar/docket-no-57171-rtp-revenue-credit-and-allocation-methodology-hearing-11-12-2026/
- Commission calendar, which shows the 12-10 committee and the 12-15 Administrative Session: https://psc.ga.gov/agendas-calendars/commission-calendar/
- Georgia Power's legal notice, first published 2026-08-24: https://psc.ga.gov/search/facts-document/?documentId=228222

**Documented arguments (sworn testimony; there are more than two sides, and the alliances cross):**
- **PIA Staff**, the PSC's public-interest advocacy staff. Testimony filed 2026-09-23: https://psc.ga.gov/search/facts-document/?documentId=228948. File: https://services.psc.ga.gov/api/v1/External/Public/Get/Document/DownloadFile/228948/109860
  - They recommend counting both the energy and the costs of take-or-pay power contracts ("Option 2") and adding other cost categories.
  - They write: "The additional $329 million of RTP revenues allocated to fuel revenues would have eliminated a $322 million under collected fuel balance. The current FCR-27 rate would have been lower resulting in a decrease in customer bills of approximately $1.35 per month."
  - They project that the gap grows each year from 2026 to 2031 (Table 6). The file is labeled "PublicDisclosure", and some table columns in it are blanked out ("……").
- **Georgia Interfaith Power & Light and three congregations**, testimony of John D. Wilson filed 2026-09-23: https://psc.ga.gov/search/facts-document/?documentId=228952. File: https://services.psc.ga.gov/api/v1/External/Public/Get/Document/DownloadFile/228952/109864
  - They argue the RTP price is too low at peak hours and that several costs are left out of the credit.
  - They say parties in the fuel case raised concerns that the method "has been applied to data center customers, resulted in higher fuel costs for other customers".
  - They support Georgia Power's "Option 1".
- **Georgia Association of Manufacturers**, testimony of Jonathan Ly filed 2026-09-23: https://psc.ga.gov/search/facts-document/?documentId=228951 (zip file).
  - They argue RTP revenue already puts downward pressure on non-RTP rates.
  - They say crediting more of it to fuel reduces the amount that offsets base rates.
  - They accept either of Georgia Power's options.
- **Georgia Power**, rebuttal filed 2026-10-27: https://psc.ga.gov/search/facts-document/?documentId=229275 (zip file).
  - It writes: "the RTP rate is by design a marginal cost-based rate that is not intended to recover all embedded costs."
  - It calls Staff's guidelines new policy and opposes energy-based allocation of firm gas-transportation (pipeline) costs.
  - It supports Option 1.
- **Resource Supply Management**, testimony of Jim Clarkson filed 2026-09-22: https://psc.ga.gov/search/facts-document/?documentId=228918. I did not read it.
- Georgia Power's motion to strike GIPL's testimony (2026-10-08) and GIPL's response (2026-10-14) are also in the docket.

**Why it stresses the process differently than HB 1009:**
- **A contested regulatory case, not a bill.** The record is sworn testimony, data requests, transcripts and briefs. The decision has a fixed date.
- **Highly technical and money-heavy.** It covers marginal-cost pricing, firm gas-transportation costs and take-or-pay contracts, which is hard for a grade 6–8 reading level. The style guide asks for "power bill", not "rate base", language. The per-bill estimate tests `{computed}`.
- **No clean two sides.** Georgia Power and GIPL back Option 1, Staff backs Option 2, and the manufacturers accept either. That is a real test for the argument explorer and for the rule "never use symmetry to equalize unequal evidence".
- **Redactions.** Blanked-out table columns test the rule that missing text is not proof of absence.
- **The record keeps moving.** Briefs come in 2026-12-02 and the vote is 2026-12-15, so the page needs dated revisions.
- **Changing decision-makers.** The order letterhead from July lists Tricia Pridemore as a commissioner, but the PSC site on 2026-11-08 lists Stan Wise instead. A Fortune headline (2026-11-06) says control of the commission changed in the 2026-11-03 election. That page returned 403, so when new members take their seats, and whether that affects the 2026-12-15 vote, is **[unverified]**.

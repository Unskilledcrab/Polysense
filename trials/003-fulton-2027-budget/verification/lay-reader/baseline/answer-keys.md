# Lay-reader answer keys (written before the baseline run, 2026-11-08)

The keys hold what each page's own cited content supports. A lay reader "gets" a question if the answer contains the
key elements and states no wrong direction or invented amount. Questions 7 (terms not understood) and 8 (perceived bias)
are recorded, not graded.

## psc-57171 (persona: a renter who pays a Georgia Power bill)

1. **What is decided, by whom:** the Georgia Public Service Commission (its five commissioners) decides how much of
   large businesses' real-time-price payments count toward fuel costs (the "fuel credit"); decision set for Dec 15, 2026.
2. **My costs:** the fuel charge on a standard-rate bill would likely go down somewhat (every proposed take-or-pay option
   raises the credit), but the same money stops helping pay base costs, so base rates could rise later (decided in the
   next base rate case). The net effect on a typical home is not estimated. Per $100 million moved: about $1.48 a month
   less in fuel charges; about $1.65 a month more if base rates rise by that much. Getting it right means: fuel charge
   down, base side possibly up later, net unknown. A wrong direction, or a single invented net figure, is a miss.
3. **Where the money goes:** real-time customers' payments are split between the fuel side (lowers other customers' fuel
   charges) and the base side (helps cover base costs); a bigger credit moves money from the base side to the fuel side.
4. **What I can do, by when:** submit a public comment to the PSC by 9:30 a.m. Dec 14, 2026 (24 hours before the Dec 15
   vote); contact the PSC by phone or email; watch the Dec 15 session.
5. **Mixed / Uncertain:** Mixed = the parties give conflicting accounts; Uncertain = the deciding facts are blacked out,
   missing or not yet known.
6. **One argument each way:** any documented one, e.g. for counting the contracts' costs (PIA Staff: standard-rate
   customers otherwise carry costs) and against a bigger credit (GAM: upward pressure on base rates).

## fulton-budget-2027 (persona: a Fulton County homeowner)

1. **What is decided, by whom:** Fulton County's 2027 budget (what the county spends in 2027); the full Board of
   Commissioners adopts it by its second regular January meeting (expected Jan 20, 2027); a Budget Commission that
   includes two commissioners prepares the proposal, due by Nov 15, 2026.
2. **My costs:** not known yet. The proposal isn't public, and the page gives no effect on a household's taxes.
   Property taxes are the General Fund's largest revenue line. Any stated tax change for the household is a miss.
3. **Where the money goes:** the General Fund (about $1.06 billion in 2026) pays for county services such as the
   sheriff and jail, courts and libraries; the dispute is over how the proposal is drafted and a 5% limit on
   departments' requests for extra money.
4. **What I can do, by when:** speak during public comment at Board meetings starting Nov 18, 2026, 10 a.m., in person or
   by Zoom (arrange through the Clerk's office); contact commissioners.
5. **Mixed / Uncertain:** the page doesn't use "Mixed". Uncertain = only the claimants' own statements back it, no record
   settles it, or the key document isn't out yet.
6. **One argument each way:** for (supporters: early input from both halves of the county; the full Board still decides);
   against (critics: two commissioners have too much say; the 5% limit keeps needs out of the draft).

## hb1009 (persona: a parent of a 10th grader)

1. **What is decided, by whom:** HB 1009, a state law signed May 5, 2026, bars public high school students from using
   phones and similar devices during the school day, including between classes; schools must comply by July 1, 2027;
   each school district sets details. Already decided by the legislature and the governor.
2. **My costs:** none stated; the law sets aside no money. It's not a money decision for a family.
3. **Where the money goes:** no money is set aside; any state funding would come through the budget (not reviewed).
4. **What I can do, by when:** ask the school district or school board how it will store phones and handle
   emergencies (before the rules start); tell state lawmakers what you think; check the school's handbook.
5. **Supported / Uncertain:** Supported = the law's text or a study designed to show cause and effect backs it;
   Uncertain = only statements, or studies that show links but not causes. ("Mixed" isn't used.)
6. **One argument each way:** for (fewer distractions, better learning; a Florida study found test score gains in year
   two); against (worries about reaching children in emergencies; evidence on well-being is uncertain).

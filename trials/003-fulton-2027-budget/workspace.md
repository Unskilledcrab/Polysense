## Layer 2: The decision

**What's being decided:** How much Fulton County will spend in 2027, and on what. [P-005, P-042] The full Board of Commissioners makes the final decision. [P-014] A four-member Budget Commission writes the draft: the county manager, the chief financial officer and two commissioners. [P-008] Until the Board adopts a budget, the draft serves as the operating budget. [P-005]

**Not published yet:** The proposed 2027 budget is due by Nov 15, 2026. [P-005, P-031] On Nov 8, the newest documents on the county's budget page were for 2026. {analysis: P-045, captured Nov 8, 2026} No date for the public hearing had been posted. {analysis: the newest notice on Nov 8 was for October's hearings (P-042)} The 2027 meeting schedule is posted only as a proposal. [P-041, P-040]

**What's different this year:**

1. A Budget Commission writes the draft, instead of the county manager alone. [P-003, P-005, P-008]
2. Its two commissioners are Commissioner Bob Ellis, for districts 1 to 3, and Vice Chair Khadijah Abdur-Rahman, for districts 4 to 6. [P-021]
3. The commission works by agreement, not by votes. [P-014] When it can't agree, the county manager decides how to present the draft. [P-014]
4. The commission can't adopt or change the budget; only the full Board can. [P-014]
5. A commissioner's resolution says departments' requests could not exceed 5% of their "base budget," and FOX 5 reported a similar limit. [P-029, P-077, P-080] We did not find the commission's written instruction. {analysis: it is not on any county page we archived; see README, D4}

## Layer 3: Options and tradeoffs

The Board must adopt a 2027 budget by its second regular meeting in January. [P-005, P-042] Until then, the draft serves as the operating budget, as presented. [P-005] So the choices are to adopt the draft as it is, change it first, or change the rules for how it is built. {analysis: based on P-005, P-047 and P-024}

### Option A: Adopt the draft as presented

- **What it does:** The Board adopts the Budget Commission's plan without changes. {analysis: the code requires a budget by the January deadline (P-005)} Until the Board acts, this plan already serves as the operating budget. [P-005]
- **Where it stands:** The plan is due by Nov 15, 2026, and was not public on Nov 8. [P-005, P-045] The vote must come by the second regular meeting in January. [P-005]
- **Who benefits:** The Board's resolution says the commission brings "earlier and ongoing input from members of the Board" from both north and south. [P-004] Who else gains depends on what the plan funds, which isn't public yet. {editorial}
- **Who pays:** Offices whose needs go beyond the 5% limit, if the plan leaves those needs out. {analysis: the limit as described in P-029} Justice leaders are asking for more money for what they describe as staffing shortages, court delays and jail crowding. [P-070]
- **What's uncertain:** What the plan contains. {editorial} Whether the vote comes in December or January; the code allows either, and justice leaders asked for January. [P-005, P-070]

### Option B: Change the draft before adopting it

- **What it does:** The Board adds, cuts or moves money before it votes. {analysis: a description of the option} The county's 2026 budget resolution says state law lets the Board adopt amounts "greater or less than the sums presented in the proposed budget." [P-047, P-046]
- **Where it stands:** Last year's budget book lists changes dated Jan 7 and Jan 21, 2026, before the vote on Jan 21. [P-054, P-046]
- **Who benefits:** Offices the Board adds money for, such as justice offices that asked for more staff. {analysis: requests in P-061, P-063, P-064 and P-066}
- **Who pays:** Taxpayers or other programs, since added spending needs more revenue, cuts elsewhere or savings. {analysis} The 2026 plan spends $73,637,614 more than it takes in. [P-054]
- **What's uncertain:** How much room 2027 revenue leaves, which the draft should show. {editorial}

### Option C: Remove the 5% limit and list every request (Resolution 26-0729, failed and filed)

- **What it does:** It would have barred any cap on what a department, agency, elected official or authority can request. [P-030] It would have required the draft to include all requests, even ones left out because of the 5% cap. [P-030, P-031]
- **Where it stands:** The motion to approve it failed on Oct 21. [P-024, P-025] The Board filed it on Nov 4, by 4 votes to 1. {computed: the roll call (P-034) lists 4 "Yea", 1 "Nay" and 1 "Did Not Vote"} Its record shows no later action. [P-025]
- **Who benefits:** The Board and the public would see every department's full request. [P-030] The resolution says listing requests gives the Board "a true picture of the departments' needs." [P-028]
- **Who pays:** The resolution itself spends no money; it changes what is asked for and shown. {analysis: based on its full text, P-027 to P-032, P-083 and P-084} Paying for more requests would still take more revenue, cuts elsewhere or savings. {analysis}
- **What's uncertain:** Whether listing every request would change what the Board funds. {editorial} The commissioners who voted no or to file it gave no reasons in the records we archived. {analysis: P-025 holds motions and votes only}

### How the budget is drafted

- From 1953 until 2018, a budget commission drafted Fulton's budget. [P-002, P-003] Its members were the Board chairman, the county manager and the chief financial officer. [P-002] In 2018, the Board gave that job to the county manager alone. [P-003] Resolution 26-0460, adopted Aug 5, 2026, brought a commission back "in a modified form." [P-004, P-017] Commissioner Ellis and Vice Chair Abdur-Rahman sponsored it. [P-016, P-017]
- The new commission's members are the county manager, the chief financial officer, Commissioner Bob Ellis (north) and Vice Chair Khadijah Abdur-Rahman (south). [P-008, P-021] The county manager and chief financial officer still prepare the budget documents and ask departments for their budget proposals. [P-012] The commission works by agreement; if it can't agree, the county manager decides how to present the draft. [P-014]
- The two commissioner members are chosen before each budget cycle at a publicly announced Board meeting. [P-009] One comes from districts 1 to 3, and one from districts 4 to 6. [P-008] A member must have served a year, and must not have a term ending in the year the budget is drafted. [P-010] The rules have a fallback if no one in a group is eligible. [P-010] An eligible vice chair serves automatically. [P-011] For the 2027 budget, Ellis was the only eligible member from districts 1 to 3. [P-021]
- The two commissioners must ask all commissioners for their budget priorities. [P-013] They take part in reviewing departments' requests, and they report to the full Board. [P-013]
- The county code says the proposal "will serve, as presented, as the operating budget until a final budget is adopted." [P-005] The operating budget is the spending plan the county runs on. {analysis: plain-language definition} The proposal covers 2027, so this applies only to 2027 spending before the Board adopts a budget. {analysis: P-005 says the proposal governs spending "for the following year," and adoption is due by the second regular meeting in January}

### How to read these cards

Every card uses the same fields, and one rule sets every evidence label. {editorial: labeling rule} "Supported" means a record that settles the point backs the claim. {editorial: labeling rule} For what a rule says, that record is the rule's own text. {editorial: labeling rule} For money, needs or effects, it is a county record written by someone other than the people making the claim, such as the budget book or a Board record. {editorial: labeling rule} "Uncertain" means only statements or requests by the people making the claim back it, no record settles it, or the key document isn't out yet. {editorial: labeling rule} "A values question" means that part of the claim is about what is fair or right, not mainly about facts. {editorial: labeling rule} When a card makes more than one claim, each part gets its own label. {editorial: labeling rule} A claim type says "with a values question" when the card also asks what is fair or right, and the label names that question. {editorial: labeling rule} "Documented" means a real person or body made the argument, and we cite where. {editorial: labeling rule} Cards are paired by topic: who drafts (1 and 2), requests and openness (3 and 4), then timing (5) and funding (6). {editorial: ordering rule} The first pair starts with the case for the current process, and the second with the case against it; the order is not a ranking. {editorial: ordering rule}

### 1. The commission adds early input, and the full Board still decides

Documented · Side: supports the current process · Claim type: factual, with a values question · Evidence: Rules: Supported (the code's text). Results: Uncertain (no draft yet). Whether all commissioners were asked for priorities: Uncertain (no record settles it). Better design: a values question <!-- nocheck -->

- **In short:** North and south Fulton would each have a commissioner shaping the draft, and the full Board still votes. {analysis: P-004, P-008 and P-014} Resolution 26-0460 calls this "earlier and ongoing input" from Board members. [P-004, P-017] The county code confirms these rules; results wait on the draft. {analysis: P-007 to P-015; the draft is due by Nov 15 (P-005)}
- **Who says it:** Commissioner Bob Ellis and Vice Chair Khadijah Abdur-Rahman sponsored Resolution 26-0460. [P-016, P-017] It says the new commission is "structured to ensure balanced representation from both the northern and southern portions of Fulton County." [P-004] It says the design preserves "the full Board's exclusive authority to adopt the County's annual budget." [P-004] It says a budget commission drafted the budget from 1953 until 2018. [P-002, P-003] Ellis said the full Board will review and vote on the final budget, FOX 5 reported. [P-079, P-080]
- **What supports it:** The code says the commission can't adopt, amend or approve the budget. [P-014] Only the full Board can. [P-014] The county manager and chief financial officer still prepare the budget documents. [P-012] The two commissioners must ask all commissioners for their budget priorities and report to the full Board. [P-013] The code says they stand for "the policy priorities of the full board of commissioners." [P-007] It also says each represents "the interests and priorities of their respective district grouping." [P-013]
- **Who disagrees:** FOX 5 reported that the justice officials' main objection is to presenting their needs to a two-commissioner panel rather than the full Board. [P-076, P-080] Commissioner Dana Barrett said the two are "creating a budget commission that only they qualify to be on." [P-072]
- **What challenges it:** The 1953-2018 commission's only Board member was the chairman, who serves at large; the new one has two district commissioners instead. [P-002, P-003, P-008, P-035] In the 2026 cycle, the budget book lists net General Fund spending changes to the county manager's proposal dated Jan 7, adding $20,597,446, and Jan 21, taking out $16,648,868. [P-054, P-047] Those changes included $18,157,000 more for consent decree costs. {computed: $50,000,000 adopted − $31,843,000 proposed (P-054)} Together, they left the adopted General Fund $3,948,578, or about 0.4%, above the proposal. {computed: $20,597,446 − $16,648,868 = $3,948,578; $3,948,578 ÷ $1,053,152,000 ≈ 0.004 (P-054)} The planned draw on savings grew from $57,102,996 to $73,637,614. [P-054] That means the proposal's General Fund spending total largely held, though some of its lines and its draw on savings changed by millions of dollars. {analysis: what the figures above show (P-054)} Commissioner Barrett's Resolution 26-0729 says the commission failed its duty to ask all commissioners for their priorities when it set the 5% cap. [P-029, P-032, P-024]
- **What's missing:** Apart from that claim, we found no record on whether the two asked every commissioner for priorities. {analysis: none in the sources reviewed; the claim is in P-029}
- **What's uncertain:** Whether the budget will differ from one drafted by the county manager alone. {editorial} The draft isn't out, and there is no other draft to compare. {analysis: P-045, captured Nov 8, 2026}

### 2. Two commissioners, seated under rules they sponsored, have too much say

Documented · Side: critical of the current process · Claim type: factual, with a values question · Evidence: Who serves in 2027, and how chosen: Supported (county records). Hearings held by the commission: Supported (county notice). How much the two shape the draft: Uncertain (no draft yet). Fairness: a values question <!-- nocheck -->

- **In short:** Department heads must present their needs to two commissioners, not the full Board, critics say. [P-076] Commissioner Barrett said the rules let only those two serve. [P-072] Who serves is on record; the two commissioners' effect on the draft isn't known yet. {analysis: P-021; the draft is due by Nov 15 (P-005)}
- **Who says it:** Chief Probate Judge Kenya Johnson spoke for the group. [P-078] Johnson said, "We respectfully decline to participate in a process that unnecessarily limits the voices of the justice system." [P-078] FOX 5 reported that Sheriff Patrick Labat criticized the two-commissioner arrangement as unfair to county agencies and taxpayers. [P-078, P-080] Labat said, "For two commissioners to take a hold of this project—hijack this process is simply unfair." [P-078] Commissioner Dana Barrett said the two are "creating a budget commission that only they qualify to be on." [P-072] Barrett also said, "There are some shenanigans among some of the current board members who are angling to take control of the budget process in a way that I feel is inappropriate." [P-072]
- **What supports it:** Ellis and Abdur-Rahman, the commission's two commissioners, sponsored the rules that decide who serves. [P-016, P-017, P-021] Under those rules, Ellis was the only eligible member from the north for the 2027 budget. [P-021] The vice chair served automatically. [P-021, P-011] Neither needed a nominating vote. [P-021] The county's notice lists the hearings as "Special called meetings of the Fulton County Budget Commission." [P-042] The two "participate directly and continuously with the county manager and chief financial officer," including in reviewing departments' requests. [P-013] The code has the commission help prepare the recommended budget it presents to the full Board. [P-013, P-015]
- **Who disagrees:** Commissioner Bob Ellis said, "They are speaking and addressing the budget commission just like a standard committee structure." [P-073] Ellis added that "all commissioners and their staff are able to attend and hear what is said." [P-073] FOX 5 reported that Ellis said the commission alone drafts the recommendations, and that the full Board will review and vote on the final budget. [P-079, P-080]
- **What challenges it:** The commission can't adopt or change the budget. [P-014] It has four members, and two are county staff: the county manager and the chief financial officer. [P-008] When it can't agree, the county manager decides how to present the draft. [P-014] The rules choose the two commissioners again before each budget cycle. [P-009] Each of the six offices had a hearing slot, and FOX 5 reported that they sent their requests in writing. [P-043, P-044, P-080]
- **What's missing:** We did not find the justice officials' letter, so their statements come from news reports. {analysis: see README, D6}
- **What's uncertain:** How much the two commissioners shape the draft, because it isn't out yet. {editorial}

### 3. The 5% limit keeps departments' full requests out of the draft

Documented · Side: critical of the current process · Claim type: factual and about what will happen, with a values question · Evidence: Code requires all requests, and the 2026 draft listed extras: Supported (code text; budget book). Conflict with the code: Uncertain (the code mentions no cap). Exact terms: Uncertain (no written instruction; undisputed). Needs above 5%: Uncertain (claimants' figures). Effect on the draft: Uncertain (not out yet). Sound budgeting: a values question <!-- nocheck -->

- **In short:** Readers of the draft wouldn't see departments' full requests. {analysis: what the card's claim would mean, if right} Resolution 26-0729 says the cap makes the county code's rule on requests "effectively" impossible to meet. [P-029, P-024] Records back part; the rest is unsettled or rests on the resolution's figures and values. {analysis: the label above names each part}
- **Who says it:** Commissioner Barrett's Resolution 26-0729 calls the cap an "unprecedented restriction." [P-029, P-032, P-024] It says across-the-board spending cuts and caps let "legacy spending" go unchecked "while new and often necessary funding is denied." [P-028] It says the Government Finance Officers Association calls such "spending cuts or caps" a sign of weak budgeting. [P-028] It cites advice from The Pew Charitable Trusts to "resist the temptation" of across-the-board cuts. [P-028] The justice officials raised concerns about the limit, FOX 5 reported. [P-077, P-080]
- **What supports it:** The code says the draft "shall include the budget requests of all of the county's departments." [P-005] In the 2026 cycle, "All submissions were received and carefully reviewed," and extra requests were "included as an appendix." [P-049, P-046] The resolution says department budgets changed by "a decrease of 4 percent to an increase of 43 percent" from FY2025 to FY2026. [P-028] It says 27 of the county's 41 General Fund departments and agencies asked for more than 5% for 2026, and 14 of them got increases above 5%. [P-029] The public defender's posted 2027 slides list six requests totaling $4,720,447. {computed: $76,981 + $3,793,366 + $755,884 + $30,000 + $58,000 + $6,216 = $4,720,447 (P-064)} That is about 3.3 times 5% of the office's 2026 budget. {computed: 5% of $28,395,566 = $1,419,778 (P-054); $4,720,447 ÷ $1,419,778 ≈ 3.3}
- **Who disagrees:** No one in the sources we reviewed disputed this directly. {analysis: none of Ellis's archived statements, or Resolution 26-0460, mentions the limit} The minutes record votes on the resolution, but no reasons (see What happened). {analysis: P-088 and P-092}
- **What challenges it:** The resolution and FOX 5 describe the limit as applying to requests for extra money. [P-029, P-077, P-080] For the 2026 budget, the county described its "Base Budget" as the current funding needed for past spending patterns, including staff. [P-086] The resolution says departments may describe broader needs, but only "in narrative form." [P-029] Outside the draft, at least one office's requests above 5% are public: the public defender's posted slides list $4,720,447 in requests. {analysis: P-057 and P-064; "above 5%" is measured against its 2026 budget, as under What supports it}
- **What's missing:** We did not find the commission's written 5% instruction. {analysis: see README, D4} We did not check the resolution's figures against budget books. {analysis: scope note} The minutes give no reasons for the limit, and we did not review the meeting video, where reasons may have been given. {analysis: P-088 to P-093; see sources.yaml, S16}
- **What's uncertain:** Whether the Nov 15 draft will show requests above 5%. {editorial} Whether a cap fits the code's rule on requests; the code mentions no cap, and no source we reviewed settles it. {analysis: P-005 to P-015; P-029 argues it does not fit} The jail agreement is the county's January 2025 agreement with the federal Department of Justice to address conditions at the county jail. [P-048] Whether the limit covers costs tied to court orders or the jail agreement, such as the sheriff's "Consent Decree Compliance" and "Court Ordered Contract" requests. {analysis: P-061; the limit's descriptions (P-029, P-077) don't say, and in 2026 consent decree money sat in its own line (P-054)}

### 4. The new process is open

### 6. Justice offices need more money

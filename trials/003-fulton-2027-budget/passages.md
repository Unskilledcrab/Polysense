### P-002 · S01 · p.1, lines 11-27 (history, 1953 to 2016)
> WHEREAS, from 1953 until 2016, Fulton County’s budgeting process was prescribed by a 1953 local law applicable to densely populated counties and enacted by the Georgia General Assembly (1953 Ga. L. 2815), which entrusted the creation and proposal of the annual budgets to a county budget commission; and WHEREAS, the budget commission consisted of the individuals holding the county positions (as they are known currently) of the chairman of the board of commissioners, the county manager, and the chief financial officer; and WHEREAS, in 2016, the Georgia General Assembly repealed the 1953 local law, which had the effect of authorizing Fulton County to establish its own budget ordinance pursuant to O.C.G.A. § 36-81-3; and WHEREAS, on June 15, 2016, Fulton County enacted a budget ordinance at Fulton County Code (“FCC”) Sec. 2-261 and 2-262 through Resolution 16-0465, to establish a process for the proposal and adoption of the budget and to ensure expenditures are made in accordance with the budget; and WHEREAS, the 2016 budget ordinance maintained the use of a budget commission composed of the chairman of the board of commissioners, the county manager, and the chief financial officer; and

### P-003 · S01 · p.2, lines 1-6 (the 2018 change)
> WHEREAS, on August 15, 2018, through Resolution 18-0577 (the “Prior Budget Resolution”), the Board of Commissioners (“BOC”) opted to replace the budget commission with a process, codified in FCC Section 2-261, where the county manager solely creates and submits the proposed budget but is directed to “include the budget requests of all of the county’s departments, agencies, elected officials, and authorities” along with the proposed budget; and

### P-004 · S01 · p.2, lines 7-14 (stated purpose of the change)
> WHEREAS, the Board determines that it is in the best interest of Fulton County to reconstitute a Budget Commission in a modified form that combines the administrative and financial expertise of the County Manager and Chief Financial Officer with earlier and ongoing input from members of the Board, structured to ensure balanced representation from both the northern and southern portions of Fulton County; and WHEREAS, the Board desires to establish clear procedures governing the composition, selection, eligibility, and duties of such Budget Commission, while preserving the full Board's exclusive authority to adopt the County's annual budget.

### P-007 · S01 · p.3, lines 24-36, new Sec. 2-263(a) Purpose
> BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the Fulton County Board of Commissioners hereby adopts a new section of the Fulton County Code, Section 2-263. – Budget commission., outlining the purpose, composition, selection, eligibility and duties of the budget commission, as follows: Section 2-263. – Budget commission. (a) Purpose. The purpose of the budget commission is to assist in the development of the county's proposed annual operating and capital budgets by combining the professional expertise of the county manager and chief financial officer with the policy priorities of the full board of commissioners, as represented by two commissioners selected in accordance with this code section.

### P-008 · S01 · pp.3-4, Sec. 2-263(b) Composition
> (b) Composition. The budget commission shall be composed of four (4) members: (1) The county manager; (2) The chief financial officer; (3) One member of the board of commissioners representing commission district 1, 2, or 3 (the “Northern Representative”); and (4) One member of the board of commissioners representing commission district 4, 5, or 6 (the “Southern Representative”).

### P-009 · S01 · p.4, lines 11-34, Sec. 2-263(c) Selection
> (c) Selection of commissioner members. (1) Prior to the commencement of each annual budget development cycle, the commissioners representing Districts 1, 2, and 3 shall meet and, by majority vote of those three members, nominate and select one of their number to serve as the Northern Representative for that cycle. (2) Prior to the commencement of each annual budget development cycle, the commissioners representing Districts 4, 5, and 6 shall meet and, by majority vote of those three members, nominate and select one of their number to serve as the Southern Representative for that cycle. (3) Selection under this code section shall occur at a regularly scheduled and duly noticed board of commissioners meeting, and the result shall be entered into the official minutes of the board of commissioners. (4) Where application of the Eligibility code subsection (d) leaves only one eligible commissioner within a district grouping described in paragraph (1) or (2) of this subsection, that commissioner shall serve as the applicable Representative without the necessity of nomination or vote.

### P-010 · S01 · pp.4-5, Sec. 2-263(d) Eligibility
> (d) Eligibility; election-year exclusion. (1) A commissioner shall have served on the board of commissioners for at least one (1) year to be eligible to serve on the budget commission. (2) A commissioner whose current term of office expires in the same calendar year in which the budget commission is developing the budget for the forthcoming fiscal year shall not be eligible to serve as the Northern Representative or Southern Representative for that budget cycle. i. By way of illustration, a commissioner whose term expires in 2026 shall not be eligible to serve on the budget commission convened to develop the Fiscal Year 2027 budget, notwithstanding that such development begins in 2026 and continues into 2027. (3) In the event that every commissioner within a district grouping described in paragraphs (c)(1) or (c)(2) is simultaneously ineligible under this section for a given budget cycle, the full board of commissioners shall, by majority vote, either (i) permit the immediately preceding Northern Representative or Southern Representative, as applicable, to continue to serve in a holdover capacity for that cycle, or (ii) select a Representative from among the sitting commissioners representing that district grouping, notwithstanding the exclusion in paragraph (2) of this subsection.

### P-011 · S01 · p.5, lines 18-35, Sec. 2-263(e) Vice chair
> (e) Vice chair. (1) If the vice chair of the board of commissioners is not, in a given budget cycle, ineligible under subsection (d), the vice chair shall automatically serve as the Northern Representative or Southern Representative, as applicable based on the vice chair’s district, without the necessity of nomination or election under subsection (c). (2) If the vice chair is ineligible under subsection (d) for a given budget cycle, the vice chair shall be excluded from service on the budget commission for that cycle in the same manner as any other commissioner, and the applicable district grouping shall select its Representative pursuant to subsection (c). (3) The district grouping not represented by the vice chair shall select its Representative pursuant to subsection (c) and subsection (d), regardless of whether the vice chair serves under paragraph (1) of this subsection.

### P-012 · S01 · p.5, lines 37-45, Sec. 2-263(f) County manager and CFO
> (f) Administrative duties of the county manager and chief financial officer. The county manager and the chief financial officer shall retain administrative responsibility for budget preparation and presentation, including preparing budget documents, soliciting budget proposals from county departments, scheduling administrative budget meetings, and hosting meetings with departments to discuss proposed budgets and priorities.

### P-013 · S01 · p.6, lines 2-23, Sec. 2-263(g) Duties of commissioner members
> (g) Duties of commissioner members. The Northern Representative and Southern Representative shall: (1) solicit budget priorities, requests, and concerns from all members of the board of commissioners, including those not serving on the budget commission, through individual meetings, written submissions, or regular check-ins; (2) represent the interests and priorities of their respective district grouping throughout the budget development process; (3) participate directly and continuously with the county manager and chief financial officer throughout the budget development process, including review of departmental requests, revenue and expenditure assumptions, specific budgetary requests from other elected officials, and capital planning; (4) report periodically to the full board of commissioners on the status of budget development; and (5) participate with the county manager and chief financial officer in preparing the recommended budget for presentation to the full board of commissioners.

### P-014 · S01 · p.6, lines 25-41, Sec. 2-263(h) Decision-making
> (h) Decision-making; advisory nature. (1) The budget commission shall operate by consensus rather than as a formal voting body as it works toward a joint budgetary presentation to the full board. (2) Where differences arise or consensus on an issue cannot be reached, the County Manager shall determine the best approach forward for presenting a proposed budget to the full board of commissioners. (3) Nothing in this code section shall be construed to grant the budget commission authority to adopt, amend, or approve the County budget. Final authority to adopt the annual budget remains exclusively with the full board of commissioners, consistent with O.C.G.A. § 36-81-1 et seq. and other applicable law.

### P-015 · S01 · pp.6-7, Sec. 2-263(i)-(j) Presentation and meeting schedule
> (i) Presentation to the board of commissioners. The recommended budget developed by the budget commission shall be presented jointly to the full board of commissioners by the county manager, the chief financial officer, the Northern Representative, and the Southern Representative, in accordance with the applicable budget calendar. (j) Meeting schedule. The budget commission shall convene on a schedule established by the county manager and chief financial officer in accordance with the applicable budget calendar.

> WHEREAS, the Fulton County Board of Commissioners recognizes that across- the-board spending cuts and spending caps are not the best method for controlling costs in the long run as they allow legacy spending in some departments to go unchecked while new and often necessary funding is denied; and WHEREAS, the Government Finance Officers Association identifies across-the- board spending cuts or caps as a symptom of weak budget development practices, stating that they are “a symptom of a budget process that does not provide a means for precisely targeted reductions”; and WHEREAS, The Pew Charitable Trusts has advised state and local leaders to “resist the temptation to make indiscriminate, across-the-board budget cuts” and instead use data and evidence to make strategic decisions about the allocation of limited public resources; and WHEREAS, Fulton County has traditionally included departmental budget “enhancement requests” in its publicly available Proposed Budget, which has allowed the Board of Commissioners to get a true picture of the departments’ needs and to make adjustments to the budget based on these requests; and WHEREAS, from FY2025 to FY2026, the percent change in Fulton County’s departmental budgets ranged from a decrease of 4 percent to an increase of 43 percent, which demonstrates the significant variation in the budgetary needs of individual departments; and

### P-029 · S04d · p.2, lines 1-17 (WHEREAS clauses on the 5% cap)
> WHEREAS, the newly established Budget Commission has restricted all departments to making budget requests that do not exceed 5% of their “base budget” for 2027 and only allows departments to describe broader needs in narrative form; and WHEREAS, during the FY2026 budget process, 27 of the county’s 41 General Fund departments and agencies submitted budget enhancement requests exceeding 5% of their base budgets and 14 of them received budget increases that exceeded 5%; and WHEREAS, Fulton County Code Section 2-261 requires the Budget Commission’s submission of the proposed budget to include the budget requests of the County’s departments, agencies, elected officials, and authorities that receive funding through the budget process; and WHEREAS, by placing the 5% cap on departmental enhancement requests, the Budget Commission is effectively making its mandate to submit a proposed budget that includes the budget requests of the County’s departments impossible; and WHEREAS, the Budget Commission failed to uphold its required duty to "solicit budget priorities, requests, and concerns from all members of the board of commissioners, including those not serving on the budget commission" in implementing this unprecedented restriction on the departments; and

### P-049 · S09b · p.8 (budget message): hearings and enhancement requests (repeated on p.19)

All submissions were received and carefully reviewed, however many included investment requests for enhanced programming with recurring costs. The volume of requests coupled with known budget pressures and financial resources resulted in many unmet requests. Working within the current financial constraints, a rules-based criterion which first categorized enhancements based on contractual, regulatory, statutory, board action, inflationary drivers and cybersecurity was utilized in evaluating additional investments above a department’s recurring base budget. ### P-076 · S21 · article, 'What we know'
> The coalition calling themselves "Justice Leaders," which includes the sheriff, district attorney, superior court clerk, solicitor general, probate court chief judge and public defender, announced Wednesday that they will not attend scheduled budget hearings starting next week. Leaders sent a joint letter and held a news conference outlining their decision to decline live presentations. Their primary objection centers on a new framework that require department heads to present critical funding needs to a two-commissioner panel rather than the full board. Under the current structure, District 2 Commissioner Bob Ellis and District 6 Commissioner Khadijah Abdur-Rahman sit on the Budget Commission. The panel evaluates department requests and makes recommendations for the final budget but does not possess final voting power.

### P-077 · S21 · article, the 5% directive and Solicitor-General Gammage
> The justice leaders also raised concerns over a directive asking all county departments to limit any request for additional funding to 5% above current allocations for the upcoming 2027 billion-dollar-plus budget. "We believe it to be our duty to call attention to what we believe to be a lack of proper investment in the entire ecosystem of the courts," said Fulton County Solicitor General Keith Gammage.

### P-078 · S21 · article, Chief Probate Judge Johnson and Sheriff Labat
> Fulton County Chief Probate Judge Kenya Johnson, speaking on behalf of the coalition, stated that the group respectfully declines to participate in a format that limits the voices of the justice system. "We respectfully decline to participate in a process that unnecessarily limits the voices of the justice system," Johnson said. Sheriff Patrick Labat criticized the two-commissioner arrangement as unfair to county agencies and taxpayers. "For two commissioners to take a hold of this project—hijack this process is simply unfair," Labat said.

### P-079 · S21 · article, Commissioner Ellis

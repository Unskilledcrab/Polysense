### P-049 · S06 · p.13, lines 4-15
> The RTP fuel credit is based on marginal replacement fuel costs as the RTP rate reflects the marginal cost of serving incremental load. This does not incorporate fixed fuel costs, which do not vary with changes in demand. Fixed fuel costs are costs that the Company will incur regardless of changes in RTP incremental load and thus cannot be increased or avoided through changes in load priced at RTP. A contribution to fixed costs is made through the capital substitution component as discussed further below. Q. CAN YOU GIVE EXAMPLES OF THESE FIXED COSTS? A. Yes. In the FCR-27 docket, fixed fuel costs related to non-dispatchable PPAs, existing unit FT costs, and FT costs associated with new generation being added to serve large loads and organic growth were identified as examples of fuel expenses not included in the RTP fuel credit calculation.

### P-050 · S06 · p.13, lines 17-25

These have also been referred to as take-or-pay contracts. These are distinguished from dispatchable PPAs in which the Company can control the output of the resource in response to changes in the marginal costs of the resource or load on the system. The Company’s renewable PPAs including solar, wind, and biomass are currently structured as non-dispatchable. The execution and certification of non-dispatchable PPAs and their associated costs are not driven by large load growth.

### P-051 · S06 · p.14, lines 2-14
> Firm Transportation costs are monthly pipeline capacity reservation payments that ensure gas pipeline transportation availability to existing Georgia Power units during all periods. ### P-052 · S06 · p.14 line 18 to p.15 line 13

In nearly all hours, the marginal running cost is greater than the average running cost of the system, which means that in virtually all periods, RTP incremental load is contributing to fixed costs. The Company has referred to this as the capital substitution component. Further, the CBL portion of an RTP customer’s load captures the fully embedded base and FCR rate, which directly includes the fixed costs. Q. YOU PREVIOUSLY MENTIONED A CAPITAL SUBSTITUTION COMPONENT, CAN YOU ELABORATE? A. Yes. The difference between lambda and the recorded fuel revenue covers two pieces: generation variable O&M and capital substitution. The amount will vary from year to year based on the spread between the marginal and average running costs of the system and the volume of load that is priced at RTP. The Company performed an analysis based on 2025 actual data and the capital substitution contribution for 2025 was $264 million.

### P-061 · S07 · pp.2-3, why a fuel credit method is needed
> Non-RTP customers, like residential and small commercial customers, are subject to separate rates to collect base revenues and fuel revenues. RTP sales are based on a single rate. RTP revenues must be allocated between base revenues and fuel revenues. The RTP Fuel Credit Methodology is used by the Company to calculate how much RTP revenue to allocate to fuel revenues. The remainder of RTP revenues are allocated to base revenues.

### P-062 · S07 · p.3, Staff's three guidelines
> Staff had three guidelines. The first guideline is that fuel related costs should correspondingly be matched with fuel revenues to cover those costs. The second is that costs recovered through the fuel clause should be recovered from all customers, and the third is that costs recovered from fuel revenues should be allocated based on energy usage (MWhs).

### P-063 · S07 · pp.3-4, costs left out of the credit
> The Company does not include the following costs in the RTP Fuel Credit Methodology calculation. • Natural gas firm transportation • Natural gas storage • Natural gas hedging gains/losses • Natural gas gains/losses on resales • Coal quality penalties and premiums • Coal rail car leases • Coal rail car shortfall/liquidated damages costs • Start-up costs for Purchased Power Agreements (PPAs) • Energy costs associated with non-dispatchable PPAs (Solar, Wind, Biomass, and Qualifying Facilities) • FCR over/under-balance carrying costs • Income Qualified Discount (IQD) • Non-subscription renewables program expenses • Corporate overhead and labor [fn2] Omitting these costs decreases the RTP revenues allocated to fuel revenues and results in non-RTP customers absorbing all these costs. This is inconsistent with Staff’s guideline that costs recovered through the fuel clause should be recovered from all customers.

### P-064 · S07 · p.4, not all costs need be included
> Yes; however, Staff does not necessarily expect that all these costs be included in the RTP Fuel Credit Methodology calculation as there are policy and cost causation arguments that suggest some of these costs could be omitted when calculating the RTP revenues credited to the fuel revenues.

### P-072 · S07 · p.13, the take-or-pay concern and Georgia Power's two options
> Staff is concerned the current RTP Fuel Credit Methodology does not include the costs of non-dispatchable PPA contracts, also referred to as Take or Pay contracts, in the calculation of RTP revenues credited to the fuel revenues. This results in less RTP revenue being credited to fuel revenues. The Company recognized this is a problem in this proceeding, and in testimony recommended either excluding both the energy and the costs of non-dispatchable PPAs from the RTP Fuel Credit calculation (Option 1) or including both the energy and the costs associated with non-dispatchable PPAs in the calculation (Option 2).

### P-073 · S07 · p.13, Staff recommends Option 2
> Staff recommends Option 2, which includes both the energy (MWhs) and costs (dollars) associated with all non-dispatchable PPAs in the Fuel Credit Methodology. ### P-121 · S11 · section II, overarching concerns
> PIA Staff’s recommendations endeavor to treat RTP load more like non-RTP load in how costs and revenues are allocated. However, the RTP rate is by design a marginal cost-based rate that is not intended to recover all embedded costs. This does not mean that RTP customers do not pay their fair share of system costs or cost to serve. Rather, the pricing for RTP is dynamic and allows participating customers to take on price risk to better manage how and when they use energy. Eligible commercial and industrial customers can respond to price signals and modify their usage on an hourly basis. RTP customers and non-RTP customers are not similarly situated and do not take service on similar rates by design. Efforts to make RTP cost and revenue allocations more like those for non-RTP customers would undermine the effectiveness and competitive value of RTP as a marginal cost-based rate.

### P-123 · S11 · section III, double recovery
> The Company would only be double recovering if it was charging customers for FT costs in both fuel and base rates; however, FT costs are charged to fuel and are not included in base revenue requirements.

### P-124 · S11 · section III, revenue on the base side
> That said, just because the full cost of FT in fuel is not directly offset by RTP fuel revenue does not mean that RTP customers are not effectively paying their share of fixed costs with the portion of RTP revenue that is allocated to base. To be clear, RTP revenue allocated to base for fixed costs (like FT) through capital substitution or other mechanisms indicates that such RTP revenue is reducing other costs recovered through base rates and effectively lowering the retail cost of service for customers in a base rate case.

### P-125 · S11 · section III, Staff's earlier allocation
> PIA Staff now recommends that the Company recover new and existing FT on a megawatt-hour (“MWh”) or energy basis. That recommendation cannot be reconciled with PIA Staff’s own FCR-27 testimony filed just five months ago, in which it allocated FT using a 1-CP allocator, a demand-based method.

### P-126 · S11 · section III, energy allocation
> As such, energy is not an appropriate allocation factor for FT because FT is fixed, driven by peak demand, and should be treated similarly to other large infrastructure expenses (generation, transmission, distribution) and allocated on a demand basis.

### P-127 · S11 · section III, demand-based allocation and customer protection
> The Company’s proposed demand-based allocation for new FT ensures that the RTP revenues booked to fuel will cover the large load causation of the FT cost independent of project materialization since the allocation will be fixed based on the planning assumptions that drove the need. PIA Staff’s recommended energy-based allocation for new FT does not provide this level of customer protection and is inconsistent with the Commission’s approach to customer protection.

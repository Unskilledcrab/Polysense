## Layer 2: The decision

**What's being decided:** Georgia Power splits money from real-time customers between its fuel account and its base account. [P-061] The part counted toward fuel, called the fuel credit, lowers what other customers pay for fuel. [P-144] The PSC is reviewing how that split is figured. [P-016] PIA Staff and intervenors asked for the review in the fuel-rate case, and Georgia Power supported and agreed to it. [P-053]

**What would change:**

1. **Take-or-pay contracts:** Leave both their cost and their energy out of the fuel credit ("Option 1", backed by Georgia Power and GIPL), or count both ("Option 2", backed by PIA Staff). [P-072, P-128, P-129]
2. **Pipeline fees:** Georgia Power, PIA Staff and GIPL each propose a different way to share them; GAM says no specific part of real-time revenue should be credited against them. [P-055, P-157, P-104, P-110]
3. **Other fuel costs:** Count costs such as coal rail car leases and corporate overhead (PIA Staff and GIPL), or leave them out (Georgia Power). [P-065, P-096, P-131]
4. **Reports:** PIA Staff recommends monthly reports on the credit starting no later than Jan 1, 2027. [P-068] Georgia Power says they aren't needed and some aren't feasible in that time. [P-132]
5. **Base side:** Any change is credited to fuel, and the matching base-side change waits for Georgia Power's next base rate case. [P-016]

### How real-time pricing works

- Business customers with a demand of at least 250 kilowatts can choose real-time pricing (RTP). [P-044, P-042] About 2,300 use the day-ahead version (RTP-DA), and about 170 use the hour-ahead version (RTP-HA), which requires at least 5 megawatts. [P-044, P-164]
- They pay standard rates for a set baseline amount of power and an hourly price for power above it. [P-043] The hourly price reflects what Georgia Power expects its marginal cost to be. [P-042] Marginal cost is the cost of making one more unit of power. {analysis: plain-language definition, not from the cited sources}
- Georgia Power says these customers take on price risk in exchange for possible savings. [P-042]
- PIA Staff says very large new customers on one tariff have no baseline, pay real-time rates and pay a monthly access charge to cover added fixed costs. [P-071] GAM says these customers may choose real-time pricing for all or part of their power. [P-168]
- Georgia Power expects most new business growth to use real-time pricing, PIA Staff said. [P-070]
- GIPL said real-time customers' prices would not change under its plan, only how the money is counted. [P-105] Georgia Power said the same about its 2010 change to the method. [P-046] Real-time customers pay standard rates on their baseline power. [P-043] So a later base-rate change would reach that part of their bills. {analysis: follows from P-043}

### Words used on this page

- **Fuel charge:** the part of a standard-rate bill that pays for fuel; its formal name is fuel cost recovery (FCR). [P-021, P-061]
- **Base rates:** the rest of the bill, which pays for power plants, power lines, other costs besides fuel, and the company's profit. {analysis: plain-language definition, not from the cited sources}
- **Base rate case:** the PSC's full review of base rates. {analysis: plain-language definition, not from the cited sources}
- **Take-or-pay contracts:** contracts, such as Georgia Power's solar, wind and biomass deals, under which it must pay for power whenever it is produced. [P-050] The filings also call them non-dispatchable PPAs, or power purchase agreements. [P-050, P-119]
- **Pipeline fees:** fixed monthly payments to reserve space on gas pipelines for Georgia Power's power plants. [P-049, P-051] The filings call them firm transportation (FT). [P-096]
- **Peak demand and energy use:** peak demand is the most power used at one time, such as on a cold winter morning; energy use is the total power used over a month. {analysis: plain-language definitions, not from the cited sources}
- **Marginal replacement fuel cost:** roughly, the fuel cost of making one more unit of power in a given hour. {analysis: plain-language gloss of P-048 and P-049}
- **Very large customers:** GAM describes new "very large load" customers as those using 100 megawatts or more. [P-168] This page also uses "very large customers" for the filings' "large load customers." {editorial}
- **Incremental load:** the power billed at real-time prices. [P-043]
- **GPC:** Georgia Power Company. [P-109]
- **Embedded costs:** the full average cost of running the system, as opposed to marginal cost. {analysis: plain-language definition, not from the cited sources}
- **kWh and MWh:** a kilowatt-hour (kWh) is the unit on a power bill, and a megawatt-hour (MWh) is 1,000 of them. {analysis: plain-language definition, not from the cited sources}

### Fuel side versus base side

- Under the PSC's order, any change is credited to fuel, and the matching base-side change is recorded in a special account to be addressed in the next base rate case. [P-016] The order does not say which way base rates would move. {analysis: P-016 provides for "a regulatory asset or liability"}
- Georgia Power says the benefits of adding large customers let it freeze base rates through 2028, and that their added revenue will put downward pressure of at least $102 a year on a typical home's bill in 2029–2031. [P-054] That figure covers all revenue from large customers, not only the real-time money at issue here. {analysis: P-054 describes "the incremental revenue from large load customers"}
- If fuel charges had to collect $100 million less, a typical home's bill would drop by about $1.48 a month, Georgia Power estimates. [P-141, P-142] If base rates had to collect $100 million more, that bill would rise by about $1.65 a month, it estimates. {computed: P-143 gives +$1.6484 a month per +$100 million of base revenue requirement, rounded to the cent} These are examples for a home using 1,000 kWh a month, based on its recent storm-cost and fuel-rate changes. ### Plan A: Keep today's method

- **What it does:** Counts real-time money toward fuel at the "average hourly marginal replacement fuel cost" of Georgia Power's running plants, plus its share of "pool sales or purchases." [P-048] Fixed fuel costs, such as take-or-pay contracts and pipeline fees, stay out of that rate. [P-049]
- **Where it stands:** Among the four parties whose testimony we could read, none asks to keep it unchanged. {analysis: Georgia Power (P-055, P-058), PIA Staff (P-073, P-084) and GIPL (P-095, P-096) propose changes; GAM (P-111) accepts one only if the PSC finds it is needed} GAM says real-time revenue already lowers other customers' rates. [P-111] It says that if the PSC finds a change is needed, either of Georgia Power's take-or-pay options would "adequately address" the concerns raised. [P-111] GIPL says Georgia Power, while agreeing to some changes, "argues that the current method is fair." [P-099]
- **Who benefits:** Customers on standard rates, Georgia Power and GAM say. [P-124, P-111] They say real-time money not counted toward fuel helps pay fixed costs on the base side, which lowers base costs. [P-124, P-111] Georgia Power estimates that one part of that money, which it calls capital substitution, came to $264 million in 2025. [P-052] PIA Staff estimates Georgia Power's profit was higher under today's method than under Staff's plan; see Plan C. [P-091]
- **Who pays:** PIA Staff says customers on standard rates absorb the fixed fuel costs left out of the credit. [P-063] PIA Staff estimates that Option 2 would have added $70 million, $74 million and $76 million to the credit in 2023, 2024 and 2025. [P-078] Staff says its data were incomplete, so these figures may be too low. [P-078]
- **What's uncertain:** Whether the real-time money on the base side makes up for the fuel costs left out. {editorial} Georgia Power and GAM say real-time money on the base side helps pay fixed costs, including pipeline fees. [P-124, P-110] GIPL agrees with the idea, but says it neglects two things: fuel and base costs are shared among customer groups differently, and fuel balances carry forward. [P-099]

[P-130] GIPL witness John D. Wilson filed direct testimony on Sep 23, 2026. ### 2. Count the costs of take-or-pay contracts too (Option 2)

Documented · Side: favors Option 2 · Claim type: factual and values · Evidence: Mixed on the premise that the contracts serve all customers, so their costs belong in the credit (PIA Staff says so; Georgia Power and GIPL say the costs and benefits belong with customers on standard rates); Uncertain on size (only PIA Staff's estimate is public) <!-- nocheck -->

### 3. Recover new pipeline fees from very large customers, split by winter peak (Georgia Power's pipeline plan)

Documented · Side: favors Georgia Power's pipeline plan · Claim type: factual and values · Evidence: Mixed on the premise that new pipeline fees are driven by peak demand from very large customers' growth (Georgia Power says so; PIA Staff says the fees are needed in every hour); Uncertain on size (the new-fee forecast is blacked out) <!-- nocheck -->

- **In short:** Georgia Power says very large new customers drive new pipeline fees, so they should cover them, split by winter peak demand growth. [P-051, P-055, P-056] PIA Staff and GIPL disagree with this split. [P-085, P-101]
- **Who says it:** Georgia Power witnesses Ross Beppler, Lee Evans and Brandon Looney filed direct testimony on Aug 14, 2026. [P-041] They proposed that new pipeline fees tied to large customers "be fully recovered from those customers and not borne by non-large load customers in the FCR clause." [P-055] In rebuttal testimony filed Oct 27, 2026, they said energy use "is not an appropriate allocation factor for FT because FT is fixed, driven by peak demand, and should be treated similarly to other large infrastructure expenses (generation, transmission, distribution) and allocated on a demand basis." [P-117, P-126]
- **What supports it:** Georgia Power said "the growth of large load customers is a primary driver of new FT costs." [P-051] It said its split builds on PIA Staff's own peak-based showing in the fuel case: "The Company agrees with this approach but proposes using a winter CP." [P-056] Georgia Power said its split is fixed in advance, so it covers the new fees tied to large customers even if their projects don't materialize. [P-127] GIPL's filed testimony also said pipeline space "is required to meet peak demand conditions." [P-104] GAM said the plan "would ensure that incremental FT costs attributable to new very large load customers are fully recovered from these customers." [P-113]
- **Who disagrees:** PIA Staff said: "FT is necessary for all hours across each month in order to reliably serve customers." [P-085] GIPL agreed that taking new large-customer fees out of the fuel charge "addresses one problem," but it rejected the winter-peak split and said real-time load should not be excused from "an explicit allocation of FT cost." [P-101]
- **What challenges it:** PIA Staff said the plan leaves out existing pipeline fees and "adds undue complexity." [P-082] Georgia Power said it treats existing fees separately because they "are not driven by large load growth." [P-051] GIPL said Georgia Power has not let intervenors review the large-load cost model, "not even on a trade secret basis." [P-103] Georgia Power said its split is "consistent with the capacity need that drove the incremental generation resources and the allocation in the large load cost model." [P-056] GAM said "No specific portion of RTP revenues should be credited against FT costs," while this plan books real-time money to fuel to cover the new fees. [P-110, P-127]
- **What's missing:** The dollar amounts in Georgia Power's forecast of new pipeline fees are blacked out in the public copy. [P-146]
- **What's uncertain:** How much new pipeline cost very large customers will cause, since those amounts are not public. {analysis: P-146} How to split fixed costs between customer groups is partly a values question. {editorial}

### 4. Share all pipeline and storage fees by energy use (PIA Staff's pipeline plan)

Documented · Side: favors PIA Staff's pipeline plan · Claim type: factual and values · Evidence: Mixed on the premise that pipeline fees serve every hour and that an energy split avoids collecting them twice (PIA Staff says so; Georgia Power disputes both); Uncertain on size (PIA Staff's yearly estimates are blacked out) <!-- nocheck -->

- **In short:** PIA Staff says pipeline and storage fees are needed every hour, so all should be shared by monthly energy use. [P-085, P-157] Georgia Power and GAM disagree with sharing the fees this way. [P-126, P-127, P-110]
- **Who says it:** PIA Staff witnesses Tom Newsome, Philip Hayet and Leah Wellborn filed testimony on Sep 23, 2026. [P-059, P-060, P-160, P-161] They said an energy split "is consistent with the manner in which fuel costs are recovered" and with "how FT costs have been recovered from non-RTP customers over the past 15 years." [P-084]
- **What supports it:** Staff's reason is the risk of double recovery. [P-083] It said Georgia Power has asserted that some base-side real-time money covers pipeline fees, while "all FT costs are indisputably charged to and recovered from fuel revenues." [P-083, P-124, P-052] Staff said its plan is meant "to eliminate this risk." [P-084] PIA Staff said real-time customers use power "in both high and low load hours." [P-081] Georgia Power's rebuttal says GIPL's witness later moved to an energy-based split. [P-154, P-155]
- **Who disagrees:** Georgia Power said Staff's energy split "does not provide this level of customer protection" if large projects fall through. [P-127] It said pipeline fees are "fixed, driven by peak demand," like other large infrastructure costs. [P-126] It said it would only collect twice "if it was charging customers for FT costs in both fuel and base rates," and that pipeline fees are not in base rates. [P-123] GAM said "No specific portion of RTP revenues should be credited against FT costs." [P-110] It said real-time sales already help recover these costs when system lambda, which GAM says reflects the replacement cost of fuel, "exceeds GPC’s average fuel costs." [P-110]
- **What challenges it:** Georgia Power said Staff's energy split "cannot be reconciled" with Staff's fuel-case testimony five months earlier, which used a peak-based method. [P-125] PIA Staff had said it used that method "simply for illustration purposes." [P-145]
- **What's missing:** PIA Staff's yearly estimates for its pipeline plan are blacked out in the public copy. [P-147]
- **What's uncertain:** Whether real-time money on the base side already helps pay these fees, as Georgia Power and GAM say. [P-124, P-110] GIPL called that "questionable." [P-148]

### 6. Count more fuel-related costs in the credit (PIA Staff and GIPL)

Documented · Side: favors counting more costs (PIA Staff and GIPL) · Claim type: factual and values · Evidence: Mixed on the premise that the costs left out fall on customers on standard rates (PIA Staff and GIPL say so; Georgia Power says real-time customers pay toward fixed costs through the base side); Uncertain on size (most single amounts are blacked out; yearly totals are public) <!-- nocheck -->

[P-092, P-152] Wilson said the credit is "an underestimate of the fuel costs that should be allocated to the incremental load priced at RTP." [P-099]
- **What supports it:** PIA Staff said costs like rail car leases are influenced by how much fuel, or what kind, the company buys. ### 7. Limit the shift of real-time money to fuel (GAM)

Documented · Side: favors limiting the shift to fuel; accepts either of Georgia Power's take-or-pay options if the PSC finds a change is needed (GAM) · Claim type: factual and predictive · Evidence: Supported that a bigger fuel credit leaves less real-time money for base costs (Georgia Power and PIA Staff describe the method this way; no party disputes it); Uncertain whether and how much base rates would rise (a future base rate case decides; GAM's 2025 total is blacked out) <!-- nocheck -->

- **In short:** GAM says real-time money already lowers other customers' base costs, so a bigger fuel credit "would put upward pressure on base rates." [P-111, P-115] The PSC's order leaves any base-rate effect to Georgia Power's next base rate case. [P-016]
- **Who says it:** GAM witness Jonathan Ly filed testimony on Sep 23, 2026. [P-153] Ly said "any increase in the amount of RTP revenues credited to GPC’s fuel account will necessarily reduce the RTP revenues currently used to offset GPC’s base revenue requirements." [P-111]
- **What supports it:** Georgia Power's direct testimony said real-time revenue above added costs puts "downward pressure on rates for all customers." [P-045] It said changing the method would change base rate revenue. [P-047] GAM said real-time revenue cut other customers' base revenues by $214 million in Georgia Power's 2022 rate case. [P-114] It said the 2025 total, which includes $264 million from system lambda, is "a significant increase." [P-114] GAM said a bigger fuel credit would help medium and large businesses more than homes, citing Georgia Power's cost study. [P-116]
- **Who disagrees:** PIA Staff said "since the fuel costs are completely the responsibility of ratepayers and the Company has no financial exposure, it is critical to get the RTP fuel credit methodology correct." [P-090] It said any base-rate impact "would be a natural consequence of revising the methodology to accurately match costs and cost recovery in the same mechanism." [P-090] GIPL, answering the same point from Georgia Power, said it agrees "with this concept," but that it "neglects two critical factors." [P-099] GIPL said fuel and base costs are shared among customer groups differently, and fuel over- or under-collections carry forward as a balance. [P-099] GIPL also said the part of real-time money that Georgia Power calls capital substitution is a needed cost of service that "does not provide further surplus to represent downward pressure on rates." [P-098, P-052]
- **What challenges it:** PIA Staff estimates Georgia Power could have absorbed the larger fuel credit from its plan in 2023–2025 while staying within the profit range the PSC allows. [P-090, P-091]
- **What's missing:** GAM's 2025 total for real-time money on the base side is blacked out in the public version. [P-112, P-114]
- **What's uncertain:** PIA Staff said "the direction and magnitude of any future impact would need to be determined in a base rate case." [P-090] The PSC's order leaves the timing to Georgia Power's next base rate case. [P-016]

### Questions to weigh

- Should part of real-time customers' payments count toward fixed fuel costs that serve all customers but don't change with hour-to-hour use, or is the money now counted on the base side enough? {editorial}
- How should the PSC weigh a lower fuel charge against a possible change in base rates, whose size and direction a future base rate case would decide? {editorial}
- How should pipeline and gas storage fees, old and new, be shared among real-time customers, very large new customers and everyone else, and should they be split by peak demand or by energy use? {editorial}
- How would each plan affect homes compared with businesses, given that customer groups pay different shares of fuel costs and base costs? {editorial}

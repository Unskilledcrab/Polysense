### Where the money comes from

1. Larger businesses on real-time prices pay Georgia Power an hourly price for power above a set baseline. [P-043, P-042, P-044]
2. Georgia Power splits those real-time payments: one part counts toward fuel costs, and the rest toward base costs. [P-061]
3. The fuel part, called the fuel credit, is recorded in the fuel account and lowers the fuel charge on standard rates. [P-144] The fuel credit is an accounting entry, not a credit on anyone's bill. {analysis: plain-language gloss of P-144}
4. A bigger fuel credit means the same number of dollars less toward base costs. [P-061, P-111]
5. The PSC's order records that base-side change for Georgia Power's next base rate case to settle. [P-016] A base rate case is the PSC's full review of base rates. {analysis: plain-language definition, not from the cited sources}

## Layer 2: The decision

**What's being decided:** Georgia Power splits what its real-time customers, larger businesses on hourly prices, pay between fuel and base costs. [P-061, P-042, P-044] The fuel part, called the fuel credit, lowers other customers' fuel charge, the part of their bills for fuel. [P-144, P-061] The fuel credit is an accounting entry, not a bill credit. {analysis: plain-language gloss of P-144} The rest helps cover base costs, paid through base rates, the rest of the bill. [P-061, P-124] The Public Service Commission (PSC), which decides fair and reasonable utility rates, is reviewing that split. [P-169, P-016]

**What would change:**

1. **Take-or-pay contracts** are deals, such as for solar and wind power, that require Georgia Power to pay for the power whenever it is produced. [P-050] Today the credit counts the contracts' energy but not their cost, which lowers the credit. [P-049, P-076, P-058] Should the credit count the contracts' cost along with their energy, drop the cost and the energy, or keep counting only the energy? {editorial} Counting the cost along with the energy would raise the credit, and so would dropping the cost and the energy. [P-058, P-115]
2. **Pipeline fees** (fixed payments Georgia Power makes to reserve gas pipeline space for its power plants): should real-time money count against the fees, and how? [P-051, P-049, P-081, P-055, P-157, P-104, P-110]
3. **Other fuel costs**, such as coal rail car leases: should the credit count those costs? [P-063, P-065]
4. **Reports:** should Georgia Power file monthly reports on the credit? [P-068, P-132]

One party's testimony, Resource Supply Management's, is a scanned image we could not read, so this page does not cover its view. {editorial: process note}

### How real-time pricing works

- Business customers with a demand of at least 250 kilowatts can choose real-time pricing (RTP). [P-044, P-042] About 2,300 use the day-ahead version (RTP-DA), and about 170 use the hour-ahead version (RTP-HA), which requires at least 5 megawatts. [P-044, P-164]
- They pay standard rates, fuel charge included, on a set baseline amount of power, and an hourly price on power above it. [P-043, P-048] The hourly price reflects what Georgia Power expects its marginal cost to be. [P-042] Marginal cost is the cost of making one more unit of power. {analysis: plain-language definition, not from the cited sources}
- So later changes to the fuel charge or base rates would reach real-time customers' baseline power. {analysis: follows from P-043 and P-048} New large-load customers on one tariff have no baseline. [P-071]
- Georgia Power says these customers take on price risk in exchange for possible savings. [P-042]
- Georgia Power expects most new business growth to use real-time pricing, said PIA Staff, which is PSC staff acting as a party in the case. [P-070, P-006] Georgia Interfaith Power & Light (GIPL), a group of congregations, says the method under review has been applied to data centers. [P-097, P-093]
- PIA Staff says new large-load customers on one rate schedule, or tariff, have no baseline and pay real-time rates. [P-071] PIA Staff says those customers also pay a monthly access charge to cover added fixed costs. [P-071] The Georgia Association of Manufacturers (GAM) is a group whose members buy large amounts of Georgia Power's electricity. [P-109] GAM says new customers of 100 megawatts and up may choose real-time pricing for all or part of their power. [P-168] GAM says those customers pay access charges that reflect Georgia Power's added costs. [P-168] The two descriptions may cover different groups. {analysis: P-071 describes customers on one tariff; P-168 describes customers of 100 MW and up}
- GIPL says a change it recommends would not change the rates real-time customers pay, "just the RTP fuel revenue credit." [P-105] Georgia Power said the same of its 2010 change to the method. [P-046] GIPL also says the real-time price is set "too low during peak hours." [P-094] GIPL says rate design is outside this case, but still recommends that Georgia Power propose new real-time pricing. [P-107]

### Fuel side versus base side

- The case is about how real-time payments are split, not the prices, so every dollar added to the fuel credit is a dollar less counted toward base revenue. [P-016, P-061, P-105, P-107, P-111] The PSC's order records that matching base-side change as a regulatory asset or liability, to be dealt with in the next base rate case. [P-016] For the plans in this case, which raise the credit, that change is a drop in base revenue. {analysis: P-016; the plans raise the credit (P-058, P-115)} How much of that drop later reaches base rates is for that base rate case to decide. {analysis: P-016 and P-090}
- Georgia Power estimates that each $100 million is about $1.48 a month on a typical home's fuel charge, or about $1.65 a month on its base rates. - GAM says a bigger fuel credit "would put upward pressure on base rates." ### Plan A: Keep today's method

[P-048] That average covers Georgia Power's running plants and its share of power-pool trades. [P-048] Take-or-pay contracts are deals, such as for solar and wind power, that Georgia Power must pay for whenever the power is produced. [P-050] Today's method counts those contracts' energy but not their cost, and leaves pipeline fees out. [P-049, P-058, P-076] Georgia Power offered two other choices for these contracts, take-or-pay choices 1 and 2, which the filings call Option 1 and Option 2. [P-058, P-128]
